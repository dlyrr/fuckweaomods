the manifesto

Somewhere there is a spreadsheet, and on that spreadsheet is a column, and in that column is a word, and the word is patched. That word is wrong. It has been wrong for six hours. Forty thousand people are looking at it right now, and the only humans on earth who can change it are busy arguing in a staff channel about whether the person who pointed it out was rude enough to deserve a mute.

This is the whole complaint, honestly. Nobody expects a volunteer moderation team to be perfect. We expect them to be reachable. Instead the loop goes: the status is stale, someone politely says the status is stale, the reply is a canned line about not backseat-moderating, the status stays stale, and then twelve hours later it quietly flips with no note and no acknowledgement that anyone was ever right about anything. The information is a service. The service has a mood.

And the rules! The rules are wonderful. The rules are whatever the last message was. Ask a question — that’s spam. Ask it again because nobody answered — that’s ban evasion of a mute you didn’t know you had. Post a screenshot proving the tracker is wrong — that’s drama. Say nothing — congratulations, you have finally understood the community guidelines. The moderation policy has one actual rule and it is do not make staff feel stupid in public, and everything else is downstream of that.

So this page exists. It is not a campaign, it is not a raid, and it is not about any one person — nobody gets named here and nobody is going to. It’s a wall. You type the thing you got muted for typing, it sits here, and nobody deletes it at 2am because they didn’t like your tone. That’s the entire feature set. Be funny, be mean about the mods as an institution, and leave real people’s names, faces and addresses out of it — the moment this turns into a list of targets it becomes exactly the thing it’s complaining about.
